ReplicationScope: Experimental replicability, effect-size heterogeneity and meta-analytic uncertainty

An open, statistics-first investigation of Many Labs 2 publicly released effect tables. Explore the question, planned analysis, sample-design requirements and limitations, while numerical results remain pending.

01 · Context

Research question

How much variation in each replicated psychological effect is attributable to laboratory differences rather than sampling error?

02 · Methods

Approach

  • Audit effect direction, estimates, variance semantics and laboratory labels.
  • Keep independent psychological contrasts separate instead of pooling them indiscriminately.
  • Use Fisher-z transforms and restricted maximum-likelihood random-effects models.
  • Inspect tau², I², prediction intervals, influence and leave-one-laboratory-out stability.

03 · Evaluation

Reported results

  • Current release is a research implementation draft, not a completed empirical study.
  • No verified sample sizes, model coefficients, effect sizes, statistical figures or p-values are available.
  • R execution, data licensing/codebook validation, diagnostics and independent numerical checks must pass before substantive findings are added.

04 · Scope

Limitations

This uses upstream processed effect summaries, not independently reconstructed raw experiments. Study-level dependence and filter selections require verification. A non-significant replication is not evidence that a true effect is zero. All current figures and estimates are withheld pending real-data execution and validation.
