Persian GPT from scratch: a Persian language model

An end-to-end Persian language-modeling experiment, from preparing a cleaned Wikipedia corpus and building a tokenizer to training a decoder-only Transformer and comparing held-out perplexity against n-gram baselines.

01 · Context

Research question

Persian has fewer openly documented from-scratch training examples than high-resource languages. The goal here was to build a transparent, reproducible baseline workflow and document what the resulting language-model metric does and does not establish.

02 · Methods

Approach

  • Cleaned and prepared 855,724 Persian Wikipedia documents, totaling about 244 million training tokens.
  • Trained a custom 32,000-token byte-pair encoding tokenizer on the prepared corpus.
  • The public model card describes a decoder-only Transformer trained in PyTorch for two epochs (7,450 optimizer steps); the exported checkpoint's parameter count remains under review.
  • Evaluated the held-out split with loss and perplexity, then compared perplexity with unigram and interpolated 5-gram baselines.

03 · Evaluation

Reported results

  • Held-out test perplexity: 14.52; validation perplexity: 14.02.
  • The held-out interpolated 5-gram baseline perplexity was 171.60, and the unigram baseline was 1,590.26.
  • The author-reported language-model metrics apply only to the documented corpus split and tokenizer; independent reproduction remains pending.

04 · Scope

Limitations

This is an experimental research portfolio model trained on Wikipedia text. It is not an instruction-following or factual question-answering system, has no retrieval grounding, and has not been independently evaluated for broad Persian fluency, safety or downstream usefulness. Perplexity does not establish factual accuracy or real-world performance. The checkpoint parameter count and output-head weight tying are unresolved pending a reproducible artifact audit.
