05 / DESIGN & STATISTICAL REASONING

READ THE STUDY, NOT ONLY THE CHARTS

What was actually studied?

This is a secondary exploratory audit of a 240-row SPSS questionnaire file. No preregistered research question, sampling protocol, group definition or validated scoring key accompanied the source. We therefore prioritize transparency, descriptive structure and diagnostic checks—not causal claims.

NOT A CONFIRMATORY STUDY
01

What the records represent

240 stored rows, 120 marked fire and 120 non. The variables describe demographic categories and responses to 76 questionnaire items; their original meaning and sampling frame remain unverified.

240 × 89
02

How we inspected them

We checked labels, missingness, ranges, raw-item sums, response repetitions, unusual values, distributions and correlations. Scores were not silently repaired or reversed.

DATA PROVENANCE / RANGE / PATTERNS
03

Why correlations are exploratory

The same full answer pattern appears in multiple rows. We show both all-row and 86-distinct-pattern correlations as a sensitivity view, not as two independent samples or causal effects.

SENSITIVITY ≠ VALID INFERENCE
04

Why significance is quarantined

Welch tests, rank-based comparisons and HC3 regression diagnostics are provided for audit completeness. Their confidence intervals and p-values are unreliable when the sampling independence assumption is unresolved.

DIAGNOSTIC ONLY
The main substantive takeaway is not that one group differs psychologically from another. It is that data provenance, respondent independence and questionnaire scoring must be established before such a conclusion is defensible.

The dataset contains item-level responses for ambiguity tolerance, emotional self-regulation, mentalization and death anxiety. 09 / INTERPRETATION & NEXT STEPS

WHAT THE EVIDENCE ALLOWS

What can we conclude?

This report is written for nonstatisticians as well as analysts: it separates descriptive observations, unverified psychological scoring and unreliable inferential claims, so readers can investigate without mistaking exploratory patterns for scientific proof.

01

The age distribution is concentrated

124 of the 240 recorded rows fall in the 30–39 age band (51.7%), and 82 fall in 40–49 (34.2%). These figures describe recorded rows, not a representative population sample.

02

The groups are numerically balanced

Each coded group has exactly 120 rows. Equal group counts do not establish independent participants or a comparable recruitment process.

03

Questionnaire answers are heavily repeated

Only 86 complete response patterns are distinct, and 119 of 120 aligned group pairs have identical answers. This is the leading integrity concern for group contrasts and model confidence intervals.

04

Scale interpretation remains provisional

Recorded total columns disagree with direct item sums in 108 rows, while 31 D-item cells are outside declared categories. Neither higher/lower psychological functioning nor causation can be assigned from these figures alone.

Statistical interpretation, explained

Select each question to learn what the analysis can and cannot tell you.

Why is a small p-value not enough?
A p-value assumes a specified sampling process, independent observations and a correctly specified test. Here, the same complete item pattern repeats across groups. A small reported p-value cannot repair the design problem.
Why show confidence intervals at all?
They help disclose how the original diagnostic calculation behaved under conventional assumptions. Because respondent independence is unresolved, these are nominal intervals, not calibrated uncertainty intervals for a population.
What do the two correlation views mean?
One view treats all 240 rows as recorded; the other retains one of each of 86 exact item-response patterns. If a relationship changes, that signals sensitivity to repetition. Neither view proves the remaining records are independent people.
What is reliability versus validity?
Raw-item Cronbach alpha describes consistency among item responses. Validity asks whether the instrument measures the intended psychological construct. Strong alpha does not establish validity or correct reverse scoring.
What should happen before publication?
Recover the study protocol, identify the fire/non groups, verify instrument forms and reverse-scoring keys, reconcile the original records, obtain participant data-sharing authorization and re-run the analysis on the verified dataset.
