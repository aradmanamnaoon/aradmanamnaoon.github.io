01 / THE PROBLEM

The spreadsheet looked ordinary.
It wasn't.

At first glance, the study had two equally sized groups and hundreds of answers. Instead of immediately testing which group scored higher, I checked whether the underlying responses made sense.

What this means: Different rows don't always mean independent answers. Spotting that distinction is part of the analysis—not a footnote.

02 / THE DISCOVERY

A big difference.
Until I checked.

The saved scores seemed to separate the two groups. But when I checked the actual answers behind those totals, the apparent gap almost disappeared.

The insight isn't that one group is better.

It's that the result changes dramatically when we question how it was calculated. A responsible analysis finds that issue before telling people what the study supposedly proves.
