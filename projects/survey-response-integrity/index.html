01 / EXPLORATORY

Zero nulls does not mean complete

What we found → The table has no literal CSV null cells, while 395,994 numeric cells carry one of the observed negative codes.

What to do → Audit coding conventions, skip patterns and blank text separately from ordinary NA checks.

02 / EXPLORATORY

Completion is a measurable issue

What we found → RQI_breakoff is 1 for 713 records, the same number as cases whose status differs from 6.

What to do → Reconcile process-quality metadata before choosing an analytic population.

03 / EXPLORATORY

Personality scores are not always populated

What we found → 710 Extraversion_mean entries are blank strings even though the CSV has zero nulls.

What to do → Convert blanks explicitly for score-specific analyses and report resulting sample sizes.

04 / EXPLORATORY

An apparent device difference is exploratory

What we found → The reported device-code group comparison needs a codebook to interpret groups 0 and 1 correctly.

What to do → Do not describe demographic or device effects until coding and sampling weights are verified.

DATA LIMITATIONS · UNRESOLVED
  • The exported CSV contains no literal nulls but uses blank strings and negative response codes extensively. Treating all these as valid survey answers is unsafe.
  • Meaning of codes -1, -2, -3, -5, -96, -97, -98 is unverified without the survey codebook; negative-coded cells were counted, not silently recoded.
  • A raking-weight variable exists, but its target population, weighting strata and intended inference are undocumented here. No population estimates claimed.
  • Device-type codes and all personality scoring keys need the source instrument. The device group comparison is diagnostic only.
  • The status and RQI_breakoff flags align numerically but the codebook must confirm their operational meaning.

The exact meanings of -1, -2, -3, -5, -96, -97, and -98 are not supplied. The published sample frame, raking calibration and codebook need verification.
